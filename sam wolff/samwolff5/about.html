Studio

Who makes this stuff

Sam Wolff, a third-floor room above a bakery on Dundas Street West, and a press across town that does not mind a millimetre of drift.

I trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people's books, and came back to my own drawing table in 2014. Since then: three self-published comics, a lot of ink, and a growing pile of risograph editions printed at a co-operative press in the east end.

Comics

Long-form books and short strips, pencilled tight, inked fast, lettered by hand.

Paintings

Oil and cold wax on linen and panel, built in thin layers and scraped back at least twice.

Drawings

Brush, dip pen and compressed charcoal — usually finished in one sitting so the first decision stays visible.

Prints

Two- and three-colour risograph editions pulled at a co-operative press in the east end.

Why the shop works like this

Because I am one person and a flat file. Rather than pretend to be a warehouse, the basket builds an order and sends it to me. I check what is actually still on the shelf, work out real postage, and send a total. It is slower and considerably more honest.

Commissions

Two slots a year, usually book covers and editorial work. 2026 is booked; 2027 opens in the autumn.

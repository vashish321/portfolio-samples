About

Sam Wolff

Work together →

I make pictures about people who are holding something in. Sometimes that takes a sheet of paper and forty minutes; sometimes it takes a hundred and twenty pages and two years.

I trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people’s books at a small press, and came back to my own drawing table in 2014. The studio sits above a bakery on Dundas Street West, which means the work smells faintly of bread and turpentine in roughly equal measure.

Method

Drawings begin with a brush loaded too heavily on purpose. There is a moment, maybe a second long, where the ink decides something before I do — the pooled edge, the dry drag at the end of a stroke — and most of what follows is a negotiation with that first mark rather than a correction of it.

Paintings run slower. Oil and cold wax on linen or birch panel, built in thin layers over six to ten weeks and scraped back at least twice. Scraping isn’t failure; it’s how a surface acquires a memory.

Books & editions

Three self-published books so far — Hollow Kings (2025), The Long Way Down (2023) and Six Panels for a Missing Hour (2022) — each printed in runs of 300 or fewer and signed by hand. Risograph editions are pulled at a co-operative press in the east end.

Working together

Two commission slots open each year, usually for book covers and editorial work. A brush-and-ink intensive runs twice a year for eight people. Schools, libraries and festivals are welcome to write about talks on narrative drawing.

Studio

Sam Wolff

Twelve years in one room above a bakery, keeping a record because the record turns out to be the method.

I trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people's books at a small press, and came back to my own drawing table in 2014. The journal started as a way of remembering what I had tried; it became the way I decide what to try next.

Everything gets logged — the ninety-minute brush drawings, the press jams, the canvases that go to the wall for a week. Reading it back is the only reliable way I have found to tell the difference between a bad day and a bad direction.

Comics

Long-form books and short strips, pencilled tight, inked fast, lettered by hand.

Paintings

Oil and cold wax on linen and panel, built in thin layers and scraped back at least twice.

Drawings

Brush, dip pen and compressed charcoal — usually finished in one sitting so the first decision stays visible.

Prints

Two- and three-colour risograph editions pulled at a co-operative press in the east end.

Working together

Two commission slots a year, usually book covers and editorial. A brush-and-ink intensive twice a year for eight people. Schools, libraries and festivals welcome.

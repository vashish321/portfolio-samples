The artist

A short biography

Sam Wolff trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people’s illustrated books at a small press, and returned to his own drawing table in 2014. The studio — a third-floor room above a bakery on Dundas Street West — has smelled faintly of bread and turpentine ever since.

The work divides into four strands that share a single preoccupation. There are the drawings: brush, dip pen and compressed charcoal on cotton rag, usually finished in one sitting so that the first decision remains visible. There are the comics, three self-published books to date, each printed in runs small enough to sign at the kitchen table.

There are the paintings, oil and cold wax built slowly and scraped back often. And there are the risograph editions, two and three colours at a time, printed at a co-operative press in the east end where a millimetre of misregistration is considered a feature rather than a fault.

Teaching

A brush-and-ink intensive runs twice a year for eight people at a time. Schools, libraries and comic festivals are welcome to write about talks on narrative drawing; enquiries go through the correspondence page.

Collections & availability

Original drawings begin at around $600, paintings by enquiry, and risograph editions from $90. Works ship worldwide, flat-packed where possible and rolled in a tube for anything over 60 centimetres.

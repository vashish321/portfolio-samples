The artist

Sam Wolff

I make pictures about people who are holding something in. Sometimes that takes a sheet of paper and forty minutes; sometimes a hundred and twenty-eight pages and two years.

I trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people's books at a small press, and came back to my own drawing table in 2014. The studio is a third-floor room above a bakery on Dundas Street West.

The work divides into four strands that share one preoccupation: what a room looks like a second or two after something has gone wrong, and the particular quiet that settles over it afterwards.

Comics

Long-form books and short strips, pencilled tight, inked fast, lettered by hand.

Paintings

Oil and cold wax on linen and panel, built in thin layers and scraped back at least twice.

Drawings

Brush, dip pen and compressed charcoal — usually finished in one sitting so the first decision stays visible.

Prints

Two- and three-colour risograph editions pulled at a co-operative press in the east end.

How the rooms are hung

This site is arranged as a walk because that is how the work is meant to be met — at a distance first, then close, with a label to read if you want one and nothing in the way if you don't.

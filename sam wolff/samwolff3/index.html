From the studio — Spring 2026

Twelve years of looking at people very closely.

Sam Wolff has kept the same working hours since 2014: brush in hand by seven, first mark before the bakery downstairs turns off its ovens. What comes out of that room is stubbornly analogue — India ink on cotton rag, oil built in thin layers over months, comic pages lettered by hand because the words should have to fit the space the drawing leaves them.

This edition collects recent plates from all four strands of the practice: the drawings, the comic pages, the paintings and the small risograph editions pulled at a co-operative press in the east end. Originals and prints are available directly from the studio.

Studio notes, March 2026

On method

An essay in three columns

The drawings begin with a brush loaded too heavily on purpose. There is a moment, perhaps a second long, in which the ink decides something before the hand does — a pooled edge, a dry drag at the end of a stroke — and almost everything that follows is a negotiation with that first mark rather than a correction of it.

Paintings run on a slower clock. Oil and cold wax on linen or birch panel, built in thin layers across six to ten weeks, scraped back at least twice. Scraping is not failure; it is how the surface acquires a memory. By the third pass the panel has opinions of its own about where the light should sit.

Comics are the opposite discipline. Thumbnails go down on cheap paper at postcard size, pencils are tight, inks are fast, and the hand lettering comes last so that the words must fit the room the drawing has left them. It is an unforgiving order of operations, and it is the only one that works.

The risograph editions started as an accident and became a habit. Two or three colours, misregistered by a millimetre or so, printed at a co-operative press by people who are cheerfully unbothered by perfection. Every pull is slightly different, which is the point.

What connects the four strands is not medium but attention: the same interest in people a second or two after something has gone wrong, and in the particular quiet that settles over a room once it has.

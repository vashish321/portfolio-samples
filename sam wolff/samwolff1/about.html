About

Sam Wolff

Get in touch →

I make pictures about people who are holding something in. Sometimes that takes a sheet of paper and forty minutes; sometimes it takes a hundred and twenty pages and two years.

I trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people’s books, and came back to my own drawing table in 2014. The studio sits above a bakery on Dundas Street West, which means the work smells faintly of bread and turpentine in roughly equal measure.

How the work is made

Drawings begin with a brush loaded too heavily on purpose. I like the moment where the ink decides something before I do — the pooled edge, the dry drag at the end of a stroke — and most of what follows is a negotiation with that first mark rather than a correction of it.

Paintings run slower. Oil and cold wax on linen or birch panel, built in thin layers over six to ten weeks, scraped back at least twice. The comics are the opposite discipline: thumbnails on cheap paper, pencils tight, inks fast, hand lettering last so the words have to fit the room the drawing left them.

Books & editions

Three self-published books so far — Hollow Kings (2025), The Long Way Down (2023) and Six Panels for a Missing Hour (2022) — printed in runs of 300 or fewer, each one signed and numbered. Risograph editions are pulled at a co-operative press in the east end, usually in twos and threes of colour, never more.

Teaching & talks

I run a small ink-and-brush intensive twice a year for eight people at a time, and I’m happy to talk to schools, libraries and comic festivals about narrative drawing. Enquiries go through the contact page.

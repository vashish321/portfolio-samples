Studio

Sam Wolff

I make pictures about people who are holding something in. Sometimes that takes a sheet of paper and forty minutes; sometimes a hundred and twenty-eight pages and two years.

I trained as an illustrator, spent six years art-directing other people's books at a small press, and came back to my own drawing table in 2014. The studio is a third-floor room above a bakery on Dundas Street West, which means the work smells faintly of bread and turpentine in roughly equal measure.

This site is organised as an archive rather than a portfolio because that is how the work actually exists: a flat file, a shelf of books, and a rack of canvases in various states of being scraped back. Sold work stays listed. The record is more useful than the sales pitch.

Comics

Long-form books and short strips, pencilled tight, inked fast, lettered by hand.

Paintings

Oil and cold wax on linen and panel, built in thin layers and scraped back at least twice.

Drawings

Brush, dip pen and compressed charcoal — usually finished in one sitting so the first decision stays visible.

Prints

Two- and three-colour risograph editions pulled at a co-operative press in the east end.

Working together

Two commission slots open each year, usually for book covers and editorial work. A brush-and-ink intensive runs twice a year for eight people at a time. Schools, libraries and festivals are welcome to write about talks on narrative drawing.
